Expected Actions
critical
Establish IV access; administer 1000 mL normal saline bolus before limb release
Establish IV access. IV fluids should be administered prior to releasing the crushed body part. Administer 1000 mL normal saline (NS) bolus. Avoid lactated Ringer's solution as it contains potassium. Crush injury without adequate fluid resuscitation develops into crush syndrome
↳ MATC: Crush Injury/Crush Syndrome
critical
Attach cardiac monitor; watch for hyperkalemia changes before and after release
Attach cardiac monitor. Obtain/interpret 12-lead EKG, if available. Carefully monitor for dysrhythmias or signs of hyperkalemia before and immediately after release of pressure and during transport (e.g., peaked T waves, wide QRS, lengthening QT interval, loss of P wave)

important

Improper Care Branch
Limb released before fluids given
Trigger: Extrication proceeded before IV fluid resuscitation and cardiac monitoring were in place, allowing an unbuffered reperfusion injury with acute hyperkalemic ECG changes and hemodynamic compromise.
The limb was released before IV fluids and monitoring were established. Reperfusion floods the circulation with potassium and acidic byproducts, producing peaked T waves, a widening QRS, and hypotension.
