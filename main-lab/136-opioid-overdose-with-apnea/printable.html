Expected Actions
critical
Don appropriate PPE and control the sharps hazard before working the patient
Don the appropriate PPE. Note that opioids have minimal vapor pressure and do not pose an exposure risk to rescuers unless aerosolized or ingested. Be aware that unsecured hypodermic needles may be on scene if the intravenous route may have been used by the patient, and that there is a higher risk of needle sticks during the management of this patient population which may also have an increased incidence of blood-borne pathogens.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
critical
Open the airway, insert an OPA, and BVM ventilate at 1 breath every 6 seconds
For apnea, provide one breath every 6 seconds adjusting based on pulse oximetry and digital capnometry or capnography (with the goal of 35-45 mmHg).
↳ MATC: Airway Management (Includes RSI)
critical
Ventilate before medicating: support ABCs before giving naloxone
Critical resuscitation (opening and/or maintaining the airway, provision of oxygen, ensuring adequate circulation) should be performed prior to naloxone administration.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
critical
Check blood glucose to rule out hypoglycemia as the cause of the altered mental status
Assess the patient for other etiologies of altered mental status including hypoxia (pulse oximetry less than 94%), hypoglycemia, hypotension, and traumatic head injury.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
critical
Naloxone 2 mg IN (adult 0.4 to 2 mg IV/IM, up to 4 mg IN), titrated to respiratory effort
Adults: The typical initial adult dose ranges between Naloxone 0.4-2 mg IV, IM, up to a dose of 4 mg IN or 5 mg ETT. The administration of the initial dose or subsequent doses can be incrementally titrated until respiratory depression is reversed.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
important
Keep ventilating while the naloxone works; use the lowest effective dose
Regardless of the doses of naloxone administered, airway management with provision of adequate oxygenation and ventilation is the primary goal in patients with confirmed or suspected opioid overdose. Opioid withdrawal: Patients with altered mental status secondary to an opioid overdose may become agitated or violent following naloxone administration due to opioid withdrawal therefore the goal is to use the lowest dose as possible to avoid precipitating withdrawal.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
supplemental
Survey the skin for a transdermal opioid patch and remove any found
Legally prescribed opioids are also manufactured as an adhesive patch for transdermal absorption, and if found, should be removed from the skin.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
Ventilated first; naloxone given
Trigger: Effective BVM ventilation was established before naloxone was given and continued while the drug took effect. Spontaneous respirations have returned and the patient is awake but agitated from precipitated withdrawal.
Two to four minutes after the naloxone the patient takes a sudden deep gasping breath, then resumes spontaneous adequate respirations. His color improves to pink and warm, his heart rate rises to 88, and his blood pressure normalizes. He opens his eyes and is immediately agitated and confused, pulling at the oxygen mask and trying to stand up. He is in acute withdrawal, and the crew now has a scene-safety and persuasion problem on top of a medical one: the reversal is temporary and he is going to want to leave.

Clinical duration of naloxone: The clinical opioid reversal effect of naloxone is limited and may end within an hour whereas opioids often have a duration of 4 hours or longer. Monitor the patient for recurrent respiratory depression and decreased mental status.

Expected Actions
critical
Confirm pulselessness and start high-quality chest compressions immediately
The most important therapies for patients suffering from cardiac arrest are prompt cardiac defibrillation for shockable rhythms and minimally interrupted effective chest compressions. Initiate chest compressions in cases with no bystander chest compressions or take over compressions from bystanders while a second rescuer is setting up the AED or defibrillator.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest (VF/VT/Asystole/PEA)
critical
Do not delay compressions for naloxone: it has no benefit in cardiac arrest
Naloxone has no benefit in the treatment of cardiac arrest. Do not delay other interventions such as chest compressions and ventilations.
↳ MATC: Opioid Poisoning/Overdose
critical
Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO early in the first or second round for this non-shockable rhythm
If feasible, IV or IO access should be obtained. Administer Epinephrine 1mg during the first or second round of compressions. Prioritize early administration of epinephrine for non-shockable rhythms.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest (VF/VT/Asystole/PEA)
critical
Ventilate at 10 per minute without interrupting compressions; treat hypoxia as the cause
BVM ventilation at 10 breaths per minute (1 breath every 10 compressions), applied during the upstroke between compressions, without interrupting the compressions.
