Expected Actions
critical
Confirm pulselessness and apnea; recognize cardiac arrest
In cases of cardiac arrest, assessments should be focused and limited to obtaining enough information to reveal the patient is pulseless. Once pulselessness is discovered, treatment should be initiated immediately, and any further history must be obtained by bystanders while treatment is ongoing.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
critical
Take over high-quality chest compressions from bystander without interruption
Initiate chest compressions in cases with no bystander chest compressions or take over compressions from bystanders while a second rescuer is setting up the AED or defibrillator.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
critical
Apply defibrillator pads and analyze rhythm without pausing compressions unnecessarily
If adequate, uninterrupted bystander CPR has been performed or if the patient arrests in front of the EMS clinicians, immediately proceed with rhythm analysis and defibrillation, if appropriate.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
critical
Defibrillate once at 120 J biphasic (Zoll first-shock energy) for coarse VF
Defibrillation dosing should follow manufacturer's recommendation in the case of biphasic defibrillators. Zoll – Adult – 120 J, 150 J, 200 J.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
critical
Resume chest compressions immediately post-shock with no pulse check for 2 minutes
Chest compressions should resume immediately after defibrillation attempts with no pauses for pulse checks for 2 minutes regardless of the rhythm displayed on the cardiac monitor.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
important
Establish IV or IO access without interrupting chest compressions
If feasible, IV or IO access should be obtained. IV or IO access without interrupting chest compressions.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
important
Manage airway with high-flow O2/BVM without interrupting compressions
The airway management strategy should not interrupt compressions. Successful resuscitation from cardiac arrest depends primarily on effective, minimally interrupted chest compressions and prompt defibrillation if the patient is in pulseless VT/VF.
↳ MATC: Cardiac Arrest
Single shock, ROSC achieved
Trigger: After one defibrillation and immediate resumption of high-quality compressions, the rhythm check at the end of the 2-minute cycle reveals an organized rhythm with a palpable carotid pulse and a sudden rise in EtCO2 — spontaneous circulation has returned.
At the end of the first 2-minute compression cycle following a single defibrillation, the rhythm check reveals an organized rhythm with a palpable carotid pulse. EtCO2 rises abruptly and the patient begins to gasp spontaneously — return of spontaneous circulation has occurred.

Expected Actions
critical
Reassess vitals and rhythm frequently; monitor closely for re-arrest
The condition of post-resuscitation patients fluctuates rapidly and continuously requiring close monitoring. A significant percentage of post-ROSC patients will re-arrest.
↳ MATC: Adult Post-ROSC Care
important
Transport to a facility capable of emergent cardiac catheterization (PCI-capable/STEMI receiving)
Post-cardiac arrest patients with evidence or interpretation consistent with ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI/acute MI) should be transported preferably to a facility capable of emergent cardiac catheterization or, as a secondary option, to a STEMI receiving facility based upon local resources and system of care.
↳ MATC: Adult Post-ROSC Care
supplemental
Avoid hyperthermia; do not apply warm blankets or excessive heat exposure
Avoid hyperthermia (temperature greater than 37.5° C or 99.5° F) by avoiding excessive environmental heat exposure, warm blankets, etc.
↳ MATC: Adult Post-ROSC Care
Improper Care Branch
Compressions delayed
Trigger: Rescuers pause chest compressions for more than 10 seconds to check a pulse or set up equipment rather than resuming immediately after the shock, allowing myocardial perfusion pressure to fall.
Rescuers pause chest compressions for a prolonged pulse check and delay resumption after the shock. Without immediate high-quality compressions, coronary perfusion pressure falls and the fibrillation waveform deteriorates from coarse to fine, reducing the likelihood of a successful subsequent shock.
