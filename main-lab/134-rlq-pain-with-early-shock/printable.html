Initial Contact
Crew finds a 28-year-old female curled on the conference room floor with sudden, severe, constant right lower quadrant pain. She is alert and oriented but pale, cool, and diaphoretic with a thready radial pulse. Her systolic pressure is 96 with a heart rate of 124: she is tachycardic with poor perfusion and is showing early shock alongside an acute abdomen. Room-air saturation is 98 percent, so supplemental oxygen is not indicated by the 94 to 98 percent target. The two problems, pain and hypoperfusion, have to be treated at the same time rather than in sequence.

Improper Care Branch
Analgesia and fluids withheld
Trigger: Neither fluid resuscitation nor analgesia was initiated during the first 15 minutes of contact. Compensated shock has progressed to decompensated shock.
Roughly 15 minutes have passed with no treatment beyond assessment. The crew withheld analgesia out of an outdated concern about masking the diagnosis and never started fluid, spending the time trying to name the exact pathology. She has slid from compensated into decompensated shock: systolic 84, heart rate 138 and thready, respirations 26 and shallow, skin clammy. She is crying, lightheaded, nauseated, and now rates the pain a 10 out of 10. Both omitted treatments are now urgent, and the opioid must be given with the caution the protocol specifies for hypotension.
