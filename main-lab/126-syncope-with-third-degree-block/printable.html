S Signs & Symptoms
Patient was standing at the kitchen counter when she suddenly lost consciousness and slumped to the floor, per her husband who witnessed the event. No shaking, no incontinence, no tongue biting. She regained consciousness within seconds and was talking by the time he reached her. She reports feeling lightheaded and 'foggy' for a minute or two beforehand but denies chest pain, palpitations prior to the event, or shortness of breath. This is her first syncopal episode. She mentions her cardiologist increased her metoprolol dose about a week ago for blood pressure control.

↳ MATC: Bradycardia
critical
Proceed quickly toward pacing rather than delaying on atropine for 3rd-degree block
Third-degree heart block or the denervated heart (as in cardiac transplant) may not respond to atropine and in these cases, proceed quickly to chronotropic agents (such as epinephrine or dopamine) or transcutaneous pacing
