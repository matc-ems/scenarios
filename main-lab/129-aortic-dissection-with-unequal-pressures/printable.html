Improper Care Branch
Aspirin given; scene time prolonged
Trigger: The presentation was mistaken for a routine acute coronary syndrome; antiplatelet therapy was given to a dissecting aorta and the on-scene workup consumed the window in which transport could have mattered. The false lumen ruptures into the pericardium and mediastinum.
Shortly after the aspirin is given the patient cries out, his pain suddenly worsens, and he becomes markedly more pale. Ten to twelve minutes into a prolonged scene time he says he feels very dizzy, his eyes roll back, and he becomes unresponsive. Blood pressure is unobtainable, the carotid pulse is gone, and agonal gasps give way to apnea. The aorta has ruptured and he is in cardiac arrest.
