Expected Actions
critical
Initiate CPAP for severe respiratory distress with developing CO2 retention
Non-invasive ventilation (NIV) should be administered for severe respiratory distress via BVM, continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) or bi-level positive airway pressure (BiPAP)
↳ MATC: Respiratory Distress (includes Bronchospasm, Pulmonary Edema)
critical
Administer albuterol/ipratropium nebulizer in-line with CPAP via T-connector
If indicated, bronchodilators should be administered in line with NIV. Nebulized medications may be administered through a CPAP or BiPAP mask. A specialized T-connector with a spring valve assembly is required to allow maintenance of positive airway pressure
↳ MATC: Airway Management (Includes RSI)
important
Titrate oxygen to a permissive-hypoxia target of SpO2 ≥ 90%, not a normal saturation
Permissive hypoxia (SPO2 ≥ 90%) may be appropriate in patients with COPD or other complex respiratory pathology. Chronic COPD patients tolerate hypoxia better, and an SPO2 of 90% may relieve their symptoms and be adequate
↳ MATC: Airway Management (Includes RSI)
important
Monitor EtCO2 trend for downward direction rather than expecting full normalization
Normalization of EtCO2. This means a downward trend in a patient with increased EtCO2. Patients who have end stage COPD may have chronically elevated EtCO2 as high as 50s-60s, and thus tolerate elevated EtCO2 better so normalization may not be a good target
↳ MATC: Airway Management (Includes RSI)

Expected Actions
critical
Maintain continuous CPAP mask seal throughout transport until hospital CPAP is ready to take over
Once initiated and patient is tolerating mask, DO NOT discontinue CPAP/BiPAP until patient is on the emergency department stretcher and hospital CPAP/BiPAP is immediately available for patient to be switched over, or physician is at bedside and requesting CPAP/BiPAP be discontinued. Breaking the mask seal causes a significant decrease in airway pressures and may lead to abrupt decompensation due to atelectasis and alveolar collapse
↳ MATC: Airway Management (Includes RSI)
